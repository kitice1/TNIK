# Structure-Based Optimization of TNIK-Targeting Peptides for Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis

## Overview

This project focuses on the computational analysis and structure-based design of TNIK-targeting peptides as potential therapeutic candidates for **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)**.

Using publicly available transcriptomic datasets and protein structural information, the project aims to investigate the biological relevance of **TNIK (TRAF2 and NCK-interacting protein kinase)** and explore peptide candidates based on the known TNIK-binding bicyclic peptide **TK7**.

The study integrates bioinformatics, structural analysis, and computational peptide design to establish a preliminary framework for target evaluation and therapeutic candidate optimization.

## Background

### Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)

Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis is a progressive interstitial lung disease characterized by excessive extracellular matrix deposition, abnormal tissue remodeling, and progressive loss of pulmonary function.

Despite the availability of antifibrotic treatments, there remains a need for novel therapeutic strategies.

### TNIK as a Potential Therapeutic Target

TNIK is a serine/threonine kinase involved in intracellular signaling pathways associated with cellular regulation and tissue remodeling.

Its potential therapeutic relevance to IPF requires evaluation of disease-associated expression patterns, biological context, and existing experimental evidence.

### Reference Peptide: TK7

TK7 is a previously reported bicyclic peptide that interacts with TNIK and exhibits inhibitory activity.

Its reported binding structure provides a reference for investigating peptide–protein interactions and exploring potential structural modifications.

## Project Objectives

1. Investigate TNIK expression patterns in IPF and control samples using publicly available transcriptomic data.
2. Review the biological and structural characteristics of TNIK.
3. Analyze reported interactions between TNIK and the reference peptide TK7.
4. Explore structure-based design strategies for novel TNIK-binding peptide candidates.
5. Establish a computational framework for comparing candidate peptides with TK7.

## Research Workflow

### 1. Disease and Target Analysis
- Retrieve publicly available IPF transcriptomic datasets from NCBI GEO.
- Perform data preprocessing and exploratory analysis.
- Compare TNIK expression between IPF and control samples.
- Investigate the biological relevance of TNIK in pulmonary fibrosis.

### 2. Protein Structure Analysis
- Retrieve TNIK structural information from the Protein Data Bank.
- Examine the reported TNIK–TK7 complex.
- Identify relevant binding-site residues and intermolecular interactions.
- Visualize protein–peptide interactions using molecular visualization software.

### 3. Peptide Design
- Use TK7 as a structural reference.
- Explore candidate peptide modifications.
- Consider potential effects on structural compatibility and target recognition.

### 4. Computational Evaluation
- Compare candidate binding configurations with the reference complex.
- Examine predicted molecular interactions.
- Assess potential structural advantages and limitations.
- Identify candidates for subsequent experimental investigation.
